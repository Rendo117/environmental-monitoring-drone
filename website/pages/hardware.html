Hardware

An ATmega328P flies the aircraft. A Raspberry Pi 4 connects it to the internet. Three transducers measure the environment: an MQ-2 metal-oxide gas sensor, a Geiger–Müller tube and an MPU-6050 inertial sensor for attitude.

All schematics on this page were redrawn for the 2026 revision with CircuiTikZ. The LaTeX sources are in hardware/schematics/src, so anyone can edit and rebuild them. Every figure is also available as PDF and SVG.

Wiring

Sensor interfaces

Geiger–Müller counter

A 555 timer switches a MOSFET into the 6 V winding of a small mains transformer used backwards. A diode–capacitor doubler raises the secondary to about 400 V for the tube. Every ionising particle triggers a gas avalanche in the tube. The resulting current pulse turns on a 2N2222, which pulls the Arduino input low for a moment. The firmware counts these falling edges for 60 one-second windows.

Several hundred volts are present on the red nets, and the capacitors hold their charge after power-off. Rate C3 and C4 for at least 1 kV and discharge them before touching the board.
